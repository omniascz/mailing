## 2. Subject matter, duration, nature and purpose

**Subject matter.** The Processor processes Personal Data on behalf of
the Controller in order to provide the Mailforge service — multichannel
messaging (email, SMS, voice, WhatsApp, push, in-app), contact
management, segmentation, automation, and analytics.

**Duration.** The processing continues for the term of the Controller's
subscription to the Mailforge service, plus a 30-day grace period for
data export, after which Personal Data is deleted as set out in §9.

**Nature.** The processing consists of: storing contact records,
delivering messages on the Controller's behalf, recording engagement
events, surfacing analytics, applying suppression / consent rules, and
operating supporting features the Controller enables (automation, AI
copywriting, etc.).

**Purpose.** The Personal Data is processed solely to operate the
Mailforge service on the Controller's documented instructions. The
Processor does not process Personal Data for its own purposes.

---

## 3. Categories of data subjects and Personal Data

**Categories of data subjects.** Recipients the Controller imports into
their Mailforge organisation — typically the Controller's customers,
subscribers, leads, employees, or members.

**Categories of Personal Data.** The Controller is responsible for the
data it uploads. Typical categories include (delete those that don't
apply):

- Contact identifiers: email address, phone number, first name, last
  name, postal address.
- Custom fields the Controller defines (loyalty tier, language
  preference, lifecycle stage, etc.).
- Engagement events generated by the recipient interacting with the
  Controller's messages: timestamps of opens, clicks, bounces,
  unsubscribes, complaints, conversions.
- Behavioral metadata captured at the moment of engagement: IP
  address, user-agent string, device type, geographic location
  inferred from IP.
- Imported consent records: timestamp, source attribution, original
  consent text snapshot (carried over from a prior platform during a
  Mailforge migration import).
- Voice-channel artifacts (only if the Controller enables the voice
  channel): voice audio, transcripts, optional call recordings.

**Special categories** (Art. 9 GDPR) are **not** processed by default.
If the Controller intends to process any special category — including
health-related, racial/ethnic, religious, or political data — they
must notify the Processor in writing before doing so, and the parties
will agree appropriate additional safeguards.

## 4. Controller obligations and instructions

The Controller represents and warrants that:

a. It has a lawful basis under Art. 6 GDPR for every category of
Personal Data it uploads to Mailforge, including a documented basis
for each marketing message it sends (typically consent under
Art. 6(1)(a) or legitimate interests under Art. 6(1)(f)).

b. Where consent is the basis, it has captured consent in a manner
that satisfies Art. 4(11) and Art. 7 (freely given, specific,
informed, unambiguous), retains records sufficient to prove
consent, and provides recipients with an easy means of withdrawal.

c. It maintains its own Article 13/14 notices and Article 30 records
of processing.

d. The instructions it gives the Processor (via the Mailforge UI,
API, or written direction to support) are lawful and proportionate.

**Instructions.** The Controller's documented instructions to the
Processor consist of:

1. The configuration the Controller sets in the Mailforge dashboard
   (organisation settings, list opt-in rules, frequency caps, quiet
   hours, suppression imports, etc.).
2. The actions the Controller initiates through the API or UI (start
   a migration, send a campaign, enrol contacts in a workflow,
   request a data export, etc.).
3. Any specific written direction the Controller sends to the
   Processor's support team and that the Processor confirms in
   writing.

The Processor will only process Personal Data on the Controller's
documented instructions, except where required by EU or Member State
law — in which case the Processor will inform the Controller before
processing, unless the law prohibits notification.

## 5. Processor obligations

The Processor commits to:

- Process Personal Data only on the Controller's documented
  instructions (§4).
- Ensure persons authorised to process the Personal Data are bound
  by confidentiality (employment contract clauses + onboarding NDA).
- Implement the technical and organisational measures listed in
  Annex A.
- Engage sub-processors only with the Controller's general authorisation
  and the notification mechanism in §6.
- Assist the Controller in responding to data-subject requests (§7)
  and in fulfilling Article 32–36 obligations (§8).
- On termination of the service, delete or return Personal Data per
  §9.
- Make available all information necessary to demonstrate compliance
  with this DPA and allow for audits (§10).

## 6. Sub-processors

The Controller gives general authorisation for the Processor to
engage the sub-processors listed at `/legal/sub-processors` (the
"sub-processor list"). The Processor will:

- Maintain the sub-processor list as the authoritative reference,
  versioned in Git so every change is reviewable.
- Notify the Controller **at least 30 days** before adding or
  replacing a sub-processor (in-app banner + email to the
  Controller's registered data-protection contact).
- During the notice period, the Controller may object in writing.
  The parties will work in good faith to resolve the objection; if
  no resolution emerges, either party may terminate the service for
  the affected functionality without penalty.
- Bind every sub-processor in writing to data-protection obligations
  no less protective than those between Processor and Controller.

## 7. Assistance with data-subject rights

The Processor will assist the Controller in responding to data-subject
requests under Articles 15–22 GDPR by providing:

- **Access** (Art. 15) — `GET /api/v1/contacts/:id/export-data` returns
  the full set of Personal Data Mailforge holds for the subject.
- **Erasure** (Art. 17) — `POST /api/v1/contacts/:id/anonymize` scrubs
  PII + behavioral metadata and records a hash-based suppression so
  future re-imports can't resurrect the identity.
- **Portability** (Art. 20) — the export endpoint above returns
  structured JSON; CSV is also available via
  `GET /api/v1/contacts/:id/activity-export`.
- **Rectification** (Art. 16) — the Controller updates profile fields
  through the dashboard or API.
- **Restriction** (Art. 18) — the Controller can set the contact's
  status to `unsubscribed` or revoke per-channel consent via the
  consent endpoints introduced in Sprint D.4.
- **Objection** (Art. 21) — handled through the public preference
  centre at `/p/center/:token`; the recipient self-serves.

Mailforge will respond to direct data-subject requests by directing
the subject to the Controller — Mailforge is the Processor, not the
Controller.

## 8. Personal data breach notification

The Processor will notify the Controller **without undue delay and in
no event later than 48 hours** after becoming aware of a Personal
Data breach affecting the Controller's data. The notification will
include:

- Nature of the breach + approximate number of data subjects + records.
- The likely consequences and the measures taken or proposed.
- The Processor's data-protection contact at `dpo@mailforge.io` for
  follow-up.

The Processor will assist the Controller in meeting the Controller's
72-hour notification obligation to its supervisory authority.

---

## 9. Return / deletion of Personal Data

On termination of the service:

- The Controller has **30 days** of read-only access to export
  Personal Data via the API or UI.
- After 30 days, the Processor permanently deletes all Personal Data
  except:
  - Personal Data subject to a continuing legal-retention obligation
    (audit logs of consent transitions, retained for 4 years per
    customary GDPR statute-of-limitations practice).
  - Personal Data preserved in tamper-evident backups, retained for a
    further 30 days before being overwritten in normal backup
    rotation.

The Processor will issue a written confirmation of deletion on
request.

---

## 10. Audits

On reasonable written notice (at least 30 days) and no more than once
per year (unless a Personal Data breach warrants an immediate audit),
the Controller — or a third-party auditor reasonably acceptable to
the Processor — may audit the Processor's compliance with this DPA.

To minimise disruption, the parties agree:

- Audits are conducted at the Processor's operational premises during
  normal business hours.
- The auditor is bound by confidentiality at least as strong as this
  DPA.
- The Processor's standard documentation (SOC 2 reports once
  available, ISO 27001 certificate, this DPA, the sub-processor
  list, and the Annex A technical-measures appendix) is the first
  evidence supplied to the auditor; a deeper investigation is taken
  only if those documents leave material questions unanswered.

## Annex A — Technical and organisational measures

### A.1 Access control

- Operator accounts use email/password + JWT session + RBAC (owner,
  admin, editor, viewer) + optional two-factor.
- The MTA cluster and database hosts are reachable only via the
  organisation's Tailscale mesh; no public SSH.
- API requests are throttled per organisation and per IP; auth
  failures lock the account after repeated attempts.

### A.2 Encryption

- TLS 1.2+ on every external HTTPS endpoint and internal gRPC.
- Database storage encryption at rest (LUKS on Hetzner Dedicated
  volumes).
- Outbound email is delivered with STARTTLS where the recipient
  server supports it; DKIM signatures are 2048-bit RSA.
- The Mailforge MTA records per-recipient TLS state for audit.

### A.3 Resilience

- Daily database backups + continuous WAL archival to Hetzner Storage
  Box; off-site replication to Cloudflare R2.
- The MTA cluster runs in two Hetzner data centres (Falkenstein +
  Helsinki); failure of one keeps sending alive.
- Quarterly disaster-recovery drill: restore the latest backup to a
  fresh host and verify integrity.

### A.4 Logging + audit trail

- Application logs (Better Stack), error tracking (Sentry EU), and
  metric aggregation (Grafana Cloud EU) are scoped to operational
  data — no recipient PII.
- Every admin action on the Mailforge dashboard is recorded in the
  `audit_logs` table for 4 years (consent transitions, mass deletes,
  permission changes).
- Per-org rate-limit + abuse-detection signals route into an internal
  alerting system; persistent abuse triggers automatic throttling
  (Sprint B.4 + abuse-detection schema).

### A.5 Personnel

- Every Mailforge employee with access to production data signs an
  NDA + completes annual data-protection training.
- Production access is gated by least-privilege role assignments;
  the on-call rotation logs every access in the audit trail.

### A.6 Sub-processor management

- The sub-processor list is the single source of truth and is
  customer-visible (§6).
- Each sub-processor has a documented DPA + EU SCCs (where
  applicable) on file with the Processor's DPO.
- Quarterly review confirms each sub-processor remains active and
  scope hasn't drifted.

### A.7 Data minimisation + retention

- Imported contacts retain the original consent timestamp on
  `customFields.imported_consent_at` so the audit trail survives
  platform migrations.
- Behavioral events (opens, clicks, bounces) are retained in
  ClickHouse for **2 years** by partition TTL; aggregate counters
  survive beyond that.
- Per-recipient IP + user-agent on `email_events` is **nulled** by
  the Art. 17 anonymise endpoint (`POST /contacts/:id/anonymize`).
- Account-level data is deleted within 30 days of subscription
  cancellation (§9).
